About

Planning for people, rooted in community.

Cities thrive when the people who live in them have a real voice in shaping them.

I'm an urban planner and project manager whose work spans community planning, public space research, and equitable development. From conducting spatial-use research with the City of Detroit's Planning & Development Department to leading participatory design fieldwork at UC San Diego's Cross-Border Community Station, I've built a practice grounded in listening first, then planning.

I bring the analytical rigor of a planner and the people-centered instincts of a community organizer. My academic work on equitable smart growth and gentrification deepened my understanding of how planning decisions, well-intentioned or not, can uplift or displace communities. That awareness drives everything I do.

Outside of planning, I've also led production and procurement operations at a Los Angeles consumer goods company, where I managed cross-functional teams, vendor relationships, and complex supply systems. That operational experience sharpened how I think about implementation, systems coordination, and getting plans actually built.

Spirit Plaza — A Public Space Pilot

Spirit Plaza was a pilot activation that closed a block of Woodward Avenue at the Spirit of Detroit statue, converting a downtown traffic corridor into a civic plaza. I joined the Planning & Development Department's data collection effort to evaluate how the space was used before and after activation, and to inform the city's decision on whether to make the plaza permanent.

My Role
I conducted intercept surveys with plaza users, completed weekday and weekend spatial-use mapping across morning, lunch, and afternoon time blocks, and contributed to the final data report submitted to PDD leadership.
Methods & Tools
Intercept surveys (pre- and post-activation) · Pedestrian counts · Behavioral mapping with posture coding (sitting, conversing, eating, photographing, food-truck queueing, etc.) · Quadrant-based site mapping · Demographic analysis · Comparative reporting
Findings from the Field
248
Survey respondents across pre- and post-activation phases
81%
Of respondents supported making Spirit Plaza permanent
~2x
Increase in Saturday pedestrian counts after activation
101
Peak weekday lunch-hour users observed in the plaza
Outcome
The data report contributed to the Planning & Development Department's evaluation of whether to make the pilot a permanent civic space. Findings demonstrated that the activation drew higher pedestrian counts and broad community support across age groups and residency status — including resident participation that grew post-activation.

Cross-Border Community Station — Divina Providencia

The Blum Summer Field Internship is a binational program of UCSD's Cross-Border Initiative, run in partnership with Colonos de Divina Providencia and the Tijuana River National Estuarine Research Reserve. Working in the canyon communities of western Tijuana, our cohort designed and delivered a community workshop on trash, erosion, and collective action — anchored in canyon-specific issues identified through fieldwork and partner relationships.

My Role
Field intern based at the Divina Community Station. Co-designed and co-facilitated the matching game — the centerpiece participatory tool of the final community workshop — with fellow interns Armando Olea and Natalie Muñoz.
Methods & Tools
Co-design with community partners · Workshop facilitation in Spanish-speaking community settings · Participatory pedagogy across age groups · Scale-of-action framing (individual / collective / institutional) · Cross-cultural and intergenerational engagement · Site-specific issue documentation
Outcome
The matching game was successfully delivered at the final community workshop in Divina Providencia, engaging children, parents, and grandparents in identifying canyon issues and discussing scaled responses. The workshop also piloted marine-debris programming for our TRNERR partners, with the matching-game format documented for future iterations.

Power, Privilege & Ethical Responses — Anti-Racism Program

In response to the murder of George Floyd in May 2020, I co-designed the Design Lab's Power, Privilege, and Ethical Responses: Anti-Racism studio series — a fourteen-session public program organized around the question "How might we design from whiteness to oneness?" Working with a program manager as the lead design pair, I helped build the program from the ground up at a moment when the institution needed it most.

My Role
Junior Designer and program co-designer. Alongside the program manager, I shaped the overall series structure, developed participant worksheets and supporting materials, conducted speaker interviews to inform session design, and contributed to workshop facilitation.
Methods & Tools
Human-centered program design · Curriculum and worksheet development · Speaker research and interviews · Anti-racist design pedagogy · Public workshop facilitation
Outcome
The series ran across fourteen public studio sessions hosted by the Design Lab, featuring scholars and practitioners on topics ranging from anti-Blackness and white fragility to environmental racism and the school-to-prison pipeline. Sessions were archived publicly and remain accessible as a resource for design and planning practitioners working on equity.
